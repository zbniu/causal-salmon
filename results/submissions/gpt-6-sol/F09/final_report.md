# Effect of arrangement Q on recipients' change

**Answer:** The supplied evidence does not establish whether Q increased the change `z` for the 600 recipients, or by how much. Their average causal effect—their mean observed change under Q minus the mean change those same units would have had without Q—is not identified.

In all 2,000 records, recipients' mean change was **14.10 points**, compared with **8.64 points** for nonrecipients, an **observed difference of +5.46 points**. This number is a descriptive comparison, **not** the answer to the causal question. Recipients also had a higher mean baseline (66.07 versus 57.35). The selection value included an unrecorded circumstance as well as baseline and random noise, and candidates were then ranked by baseline. Thus neither the raw comparison nor adjustment for the observed baseline can establish the missing counterfactual mean for recipients. The independent random component was not recorded and does not make final receipt random.

The only assumption-free numerical statement about the requested average effect from the supplied scale is a wide logical bound: **−19.83 to +80.17 points**. This follows because each recipient's unobserved follow-up without Q could be anywhere from 0 to 100; their observed mean follow-up was 80.17. These are logical limits, not an estimated effect or a confidence interval. For example, a zero effect and a positive five-point effect for every recipient are each compatible with the observed recipients' measurements and the assignment information. Hence the data cannot determine the sign, much less a definite magnitude, of the effect on recipients.
