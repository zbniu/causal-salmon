# Tutoring class and score gain

**Answer:** Attending the tutoring class increased score gain for the students who attended it by an estimated **1.11 points on average**. This is the treated students' mean gain (11.10 points) minus the nonattendees' mean gain (9.99 points), using all 2,000 students (600 attendees and 1,400 nonattendees).

The attendance decision was a random draw, all assigned students attended, and the study reports no spillovers or missing outcomes. Thus the difference in means is an unbiased randomization-based estimate of the average causal effect for the attendees, although their exact counterfactual gains cannot be observed. A conventional Welch 95% confidence interval for the difference is **0.69 to 1.54 points** (two-sided p = 3.55 × 10⁻⁷). The positive interval provides strong evidence that the class increased average score gain. The interval describes sampling/randomization uncertainty under the usual independent-outcome approximation; the point estimate, rather than an exactly known effect for these specific students, answers the question.

These results establish an average effect, not that every attendee benefited. The supplied data do not reveal individual causal effects.
