# Tutoring class and score gain

**Answer.** For the 600 students who attended, the estimated average effect of attending the tutoring class on score gain `z` is **+1.21 points** (95% bootstrap interval **+0.81 to +1.63 points**). The data support a positive average effect for attendees under the study's randomized application mechanism, though each student's unobserved gain without tutoring prevents an exact causal effect from being observed.

The analysis uses all 2,000 records (600 attendees and 1,400 nonattendees; no missing values). The primary estimate fits a cubic spline of gain against start-of-term score `y` among nonattendees, predicts each attendee's gain without tutoring at that attendee's `y`, and averages observed gain minus predicted gain. It thereby compares students at similar baseline scores and targets the **average treatment effect on the treated**, not an effect for all students. A 2,000-resample bootstrap produced the interval above. Nonattendees span every attendee's observed score range (attendees: 57.36–74.98; nonattendees: 45.01–75.00); each attendee's nearest nonattendee is within 0.12 baseline points.

The application score included a separately generated random component unrelated to student characteristics. Conditional on baseline score, this supplies the attendance variation used for the comparison, provided the application process itself has no separate effect on gain. Admission by baseline score means the unadjusted comparison is misleading: attendees averaged 12.83 gain points versus 9.38 for nonattendees, a raw difference of 3.45 points, while their baseline means were 66.84 versus 57.23. Alternative spline fits yielded 1.20–1.22 points; comparisons with one, five, and ten closest nonattendees by baseline score yielded 1.12, 1.09, and 1.08 points, respectively. These estimates support a modest positive average effect, with model and matching choices accounting for some uncertainty beyond the reported bootstrap interval.

The effect is an estimate for the actual attendees. The supplied material does not reveal individual counterfactual gains, and it does not establish that every attendee benefited.
