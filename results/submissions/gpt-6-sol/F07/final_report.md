# Effect of arrangement Q on its recipients

**Answer:** Receiving arrangement Q increased the change `z` for the 600 units that received it by an estimated **1.18 points on average**. An approximate 95% bootstrap interval is **0.76 to 1.60 points**. This is the estimated average effect *among recipients*, which is the research question; it is not an estimate for all 2,000 units.

The observed average change among recipients was 12.83 points. I estimated that their average change without Q would have been 11.65 points, giving 12.83 − 11.65 = **1.18 points**. To estimate the latter, I fit a cubic spline of `z` against baseline `y` using all 1,400 untreated units, then predicted untreated change at each recipient's own baseline and averaged those predictions. The interval resamples treated and untreated units separately 2,000 times and refits that model.

This comparison adjusts for baseline because assignment favored units with higher `y`, and baseline also predicts change. The unadjusted difference between treated and untreated average changes is 3.45 points and does **not** answer the causal question. The study states that the additional random selection component is unrelated to unit characteristics and that no other characteristic enters assignment. Together with no switching or interference, this supports comparing untreated and treated units at the same baseline. In the supplied sample, 575 untreated units have baselines within the recipients' baseline range (57.36–74.98), so the estimate does not require extrapolating beyond the untreated baseline range.

The estimate is stable to reasonable modeling choices: alternative spline fits give 1.19–1.22 points, linear through cubic control regressions give 1.17–1.21, and five-nearest-baseline matching gives 1.09. The interval quantifies sampling and model-fitting variation under the chosen spline and a unit-level bootstrap; it does not make the unobserved individual effects known or rule out every modeling error. The data support a positive *average* effect for recipients, not a claim that Q helped every recipient.
