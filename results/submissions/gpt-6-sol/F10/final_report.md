# Tutoring class and score gain

**Answer to the research question:** The supplied material does not establish whether attending the class increased score gain for the 600 students who attended, or by how many points. Their average causal effect (the mean difference between each attendee's gain with tutoring and the gain that same attendee would have had without it) is not identified.

I analyzed all 2,000 complete records. The 600 attendees gained **14.10 points** on average, versus **8.64 points** among the other 1,400 students. The observed difference is **+5.46 points**. This is a descriptive difference, **not** an estimate that answers the causal research question. Attendees had higher starting scores on average (66.07 versus 57.35), and admission depended on application status, which was affected by unrecorded circumstances. Adjusting for the recorded starting score alone does not resolve that selection.

The lowest starting score among attendees was 56.995. Below this value nobody attended; above it, the data mix attendees with nonapplicants. A local linear comparison across this cutoff (triangular weights, bandwidth 8 score points) estimated a gain discontinuity of −0.21 points and an attendance discontinuity of 0.405, giving a ratio of **−0.52 points** (approximate robust standard error 1.23). Ratios across bandwidths 3, 5, 8, 10, 15 and 20 were −0.53, −0.25, −0.52, −0.21, +0.70 and +1.07 points. A causal reading of such a cutoff ratio requires additional continuity assumptions, and would concern students near the cutoff rather than the effect for all attendees.

The unknown no-tutoring outcomes for attendees prevent a definite sign or magnitude for their average effect. Even using the test's 0–100 final-score range, the attendees' observed average final score of 80.17 yields only a broad logical range of **−19.83 to +80.17 points** for their average effect. This range includes both a decrease and an increase. It is a bound, not an effect estimate or a confidence interval.
