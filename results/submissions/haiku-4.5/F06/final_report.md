# Analysis of Tutoring Class Effect on Student Score Gains

## Research Question
For the students who attended the tutoring class, did attending the tutoring class increase their score gain? If so, by how much?

## Answer
**Yes, attending the tutoring class increased score gains. The average score gain for students who attended tutoring was approximately 0.85 points higher than for those who did not attend.**

## Key Findings

### Treatment Effect
- **Mean score gain for students who attended tutoring:** 10.76 points
- **Mean score gain for students who did not attend tutoring:** 9.91 points
- **Difference (treatment effect):** 0.85 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [0.44, 1.25] points

### Statistical Significance
- **t-statistic:** 4.10
- **p-value:** 0.000042
- **Conclusion:** The effect is highly statistically significant at the 0.05 level (p < 0.001)

## Supporting Evidence

### Sample Characteristics
The analysis was based on complete data for all 2000 students:
- Students who attended tutoring: 600
- Students who did not attend tutoring: 1400
- No missing data in any variables

### Study Design Validity
The study design supports causal inference about the tutoring effect because:
1. **Random assignment:** The 600 students who attended tutoring were selected by random draw (Section 3)
2. **Consistent implementation:** The tutoring class was identical for all students and no students switched treatment status (Section 4)
3. **Proper measurement:** All score gains were measured consistently and the people recording end-of-term scores did not know treatment status (Section 4)
4. **Complete data:** All 2000 students are included with no missing values (Section 4)

## Interpretation
The evidence strongly supports the conclusion that the tutoring class increased student score gains. On average, students who attended the tutoring class gained about 0.85 additional points on the 0-100 scale compared to those who did not attend. Given the 95% confidence interval, we can be confident the true effect lies between 0.44 and 1.25 points. The extremely small p-value (0.000042) indicates this result is very unlikely to have occurred by chance if tutoring had no effect.

## Limitations
While the random assignment supports causal inference, the magnitude of the effect is modest relative to the standard deviation of score gains in both groups (approximately 4.3 points), suggesting the tutoring class provides meaningful but not transformative improvement.
